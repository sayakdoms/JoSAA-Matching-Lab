Innovation 01

Institute-wide Defense Services quota

Two preferential DS seats across all programs of an IIT constitute a nonnested common quota.

1 · Phantom spots
Run DA with two temporary DS virtual allocations per institute.
2 · Rejection chains
Move a DS candidate into the selected open-category program; displace its lowest-priority incumbent, who applies elsewhere.
3 · Fallback
If the chain loops back, roll back and create a supernumerary seat.

Innovation 02

Dereservation without rewriting code

Move unused OBC seat capacity into the open pool, rerun the core allocation algorithm, and repeat until convergence. Move the slider to model one transfer.

Illustrative capacity arithmetic only. The actual procedure reruns DA after each capacity update. OBC assignments are weakly nonincreasing; it does not follow that OBC vacancies never increase.

Managerial takeaway

Algorithmic efficiency needs good incentives

Centralization prevented duplicate offers and improved IIT vacancies. Late withdrawals and no-shows still caused non-IIT vacancies, underscoring the importance of participant incentives, operational rules, and transparency.

Research: Baswana, S., Chakrabarti, P. P., Chandran, S., Kanoria, Y., & Patange, U. (2019). Centralized Admissions for Engineering Colleges in India. INFORMS Journal of Applied Analytics, 49(5), 338–354. Read the paper.
