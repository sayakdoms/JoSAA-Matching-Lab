# Research outcomes and evidence

Source: Baswana et al. (2019), *Centralized Admissions for Engineering Colleges in India*, INFORMS Journal of Applied Analytics 49(5):338–354.

## Like-for-like IIT vacancies (Table 2)

| Year | Vacancies at IITs existing in 2014 | Approximate reduction versus 2014 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2014 | 587 | baseline |
| 2015 | 286 | 51% |
| 2016 | 159 | 73% |
| 2017 | 163 | 72% |

Note: the full institute totals differ because additional IITs entered the system. The 2016 Withdraw option contributed to the additional reduction; do not attribute all improvements solely to the DA algorithm.

## Counterfactual (2017)

The authors simulated the legacy separate IIT / non-IIT allocation using observed integrated preferences and estimated **629 additional vacant IIT seats** under the old procedure. **3,672 candidates** received more preferred programs under joint allocation.

This is model-based counterfactual evidence, not randomized experimental identification. The study also reports substantial persistent vacancy problems among non-IIT institutions, especially from late withdrawals, rejected offers, and no-shows.
